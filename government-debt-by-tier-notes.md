# Extending the debt history to other levels of government

## Question

Would it be possible to give the same treatment to second-tier
(state/territory) and third-tier (local government) debt?

## Answer

Yes, technically it is possible, but it would be a significantly more
difficult extension than the Commonwealth page.

State and territory debt could probably be added using ABS Government Finance
Statistics, supplemented where necessary by official state and territory
Treasury budget papers. A reasonably comparable national series may be
possible, although the historical coverage would probably be shorter than the
current 1970-71 to 2024-25 Commonwealth series.

Local-government debt is more challenging. Official data may support a
national aggregate, but a consistent 50-year series with the same definitions,
debt measures, budget balances and interest data is unlikely. Individual
councils also vary substantially in their accounting and borrowing practices.

The key methodological decisions would be:

- General government only, or the entire public sector including
  government-owned corporations
- Gross debt, net debt, or borrowings only
- Whether to consolidate debt held between governments to avoid double-counting
- Whether state debt is shown against Australian GDP or each state's GSP
- Whether local government is shown only as a national aggregate
- How to handle different reporting dates and accounting standards

The most defensible design would probably be a separate **All levels of
government** view using official ABS aggregates:

- Commonwealth
- State and territory
- Local government
- Consolidated public sector, where available

It would be sensible to start with state and territory aggregate data, likely
over a shorter comparable period, and treat local-government data as a
separate national aggregate with explicit limitations rather than pretending
it has the same historical precision as the Commonwealth series.
