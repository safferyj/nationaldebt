What "national debt" means here: the face value of Australian Government Securities on issue, often called gross debt. It is a Commonwealth government debt amount measured at a point in time, not household debt and not Australia's external debt. External debt is the total debt owed by Australian residents—including governments, banks, businesses and households—to lenders outside Australia. Net debt is shown separately.

Gross debt

The face value of Australian Government Securities (AGS) on issue. It is a debt amount measured at 30 June.

Net debt

Debt after selected financial assets, such as cash and investments, have been subtracted. In plain language, it shows how much debt remains after those assets help offset it.

Underlying cash balance

Positive (+) = surplus; negative (-) = deficit. It is the annual cash budget result, measured over a year rather than at a single point in time.

Abbreviations: PBO means Parliamentary Budget Office; AGS means Australian Government Securities. Nominal means the amounts are not adjusted for inflation. Inflation-adjusted means earlier amounts are converted to 2024-25 purchasing power using the ABS Consumer Price Index.

How to read it

Debt: measured at 30 June.

Budget balance: measured over the financial year; positive (+) = surplus, negative (-) = deficit.

Debt burden: on this page, gross or net debt as a percentage of nominal GDP; it is not the annual interest cost of the debt.

Politics: PM and Treasurer are the office-holders on 30 June.

Gross and net debt are amounts measured at 30 June; underlying cash, fiscal balance and interest series are annual flows; debt changes compare consecutive debt amounts and are not the Budget surplus or deficit. Debt amounts use June-quarter CPI and flows use financial-year-average CPI. Results are shown per capita using ABS population at 30 June; the fiscal balance is unavailable before 1996-97. Interest lines are off by default.
